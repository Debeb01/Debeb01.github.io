Senior Capstone Abstract

Developing a Life Jacket Delivery System for UAVs

The purpose of this study was to develop and evaluate a lightweight life jacket delivery system using UAVs to improve response times in drowning emergencies. Existing drowning prevention technologies, such as CCTV surveillance and traditional flotation delivery, are limited in mobility and have difficulty reaching victims in remote areas. To address these gaps, this research designed a servo-actuated payload release mechanism to securely carry and accurately deploy flotation devices from a multirotor UAV. The design followed an iterative process, with each iteration modeled in SOLIDWORKS. After a final design was chosen based on weight, estimated performance and estimated durability, it was tested in the lab with a payload. The final design used a linkage-based latch with an MG996R servo and successfully released a life jacket in a flight test. To estimate the mechanism's carrying capacity, finite element analysis was performed on the final CAD assembly, showing that the linkage bar reached its allowable stress at a load of 6.506 kg, well below the hypothesized 25 kg limit. This system demonstrates a viable, versatile solution for aerial flotation delivery and supports further development of UAV rescue technologies.
